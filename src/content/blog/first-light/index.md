In astronomy, *first light* is the moment a new telescope opens its eye and records its first image. It is rarely the sharpest picture the instrument will ever take — but it is the one that proves everything is aligned and pointed the right way. This is that post.

<!--more-->

If you arrived through the front door, you met a maze: a small puzzle that keeps the landing page to myself and the crawlers guessing. This is the other side of that coin. The blog is meant to be found, read, and linked — openly indexed, no riddles.

## What this is for

I build things — some of them move, some of them point at the sky — and I keep needing a place to write the process down: the dead ends, the fixes that finally worked, and the ideas I don't want to lose. That is what this is.

I expect it to wander. Some posts will be worked examples, some will be short notes I want to be able to find again, and some will just be things I thought were worth keeping.

## Say hello

There is a comments box at the bottom of every post now, backed by GitHub Discussions. If something here was useful, wrong, or reminded you of something — leave a note. First light is better with company.
